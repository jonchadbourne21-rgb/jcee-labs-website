# Recovering Work Without Losing the Permission Boundary

Engineering blog · JCEE WorldLab · 2026-11-05 · Version 1.0

Our November engineering update adds native interruption tests, installed client qualification and hosted evidence while keeping the full integration gate open.

## Recovery must answer more than whether to retry

When a connection fails, the external system may have completed the action, rejected it or left it unresolved. Useful recovery needs to distinguish those outcomes and establish whether permission still covers any unfinished work.

Since the October 19 update, JCEE has evaluated additional native database interruptions, including lost completion acknowledgements. Eight selected scenarios were recorded under declared test conditions. These are bounded reference results, not a promise about every provider.

## Interfaces that can be used and checked

Private Python and TypeScript clients, a command-line interface and an installed service now have completed component qualification for execution, reconciliation and evidence access. The November 4 evaluation passed 110 installed-interface checks, with three local synthetic effects and no hosted changes.

Separate hosted work established real account authentication and persistence across an actual service restart. It also retained evidence from six selected hosted cases. Those results remain attached to their own environments; we do not add their test counts into one assurance score.

## The connected gate is still ahead

The 20-case composite integration candidate is executable and frozen for independent review. It has not been admitted or run. The exact runtime inputs and independent acceptance still have to be established.

Production readiness, physical commitment qualification and enforcement against compromised trusted credentials remain outside these results. The engineering advances are real, and the remaining requirements stay explicit.

## What this means for a workflow

The practical question is whether a team can reconstruct an uncertain action and decide the next permitted step from reliable records. That question can be evaluated for a particular workflow without replacing its competent native controls.

Our public record describes the observations, their scope and the next gate. Private implementation and review materials are handled through separately scoped disclosure.

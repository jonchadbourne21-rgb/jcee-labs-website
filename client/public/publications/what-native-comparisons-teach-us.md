## Give existing systems a fair comparison

Organizations already use databases, access controls and operating procedures to govern consequential work. A proposed assurance layer needs to be evaluated against a competent use of those mechanisms.

A recent eight-case native database comparison matched expected outcomes and native effect counts in both the JCEE and conventional approaches across two runs. That comparison did not establish JCEE superiority.

## A shared boundary is still a boundary

Both approaches retained a limitation involving compromised trusted credentials. A record that detects a mismatch is valuable evidence, but detection must not be described as prevention or reversal.

Other selected research branches also produced ties, failed differentiation or negative results. The public registry retains them alongside bounded successes.

## Ask the next useful question

A comparison can narrow the product question: does the integration reduce investigation work, preserve evidence across handoffs or improve recovery in a particular environment? Those benefits require their own measurements.

Our first engagement remains a scoped workflow assessment. Customer savings, production safety and operational superiority are not inferred from synthetic tests.

## Keep the position proportionate

The formal research position is similarly bounded. A machine-checked model can clarify obligations without establishing a new universal mechanism or patent priority. Prior-art findings constrain what we claim.

The goal is a useful, examinable result with a clear scope. A tie, closed branch or unresolved question remains part of that record.
